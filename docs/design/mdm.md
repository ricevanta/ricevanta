# MDM design

How Ricevanta manages devices: the device record and its lifecycle, inventory, software, configuration, OS updates, compliance, remote actions, and the native Apple MDM and Windows OMA-DM servers beside the agent. The feature list is in `../blueprint.md` section 3.1. Enrollment and certificates are in `pki.md`; the agent's internals in `agent.md`; the server's modules in `backend.md`; the `Baseline` resource and the compliance computation in `../specs/baseline.md`; signed commands and the action journal in `../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 7; the osquery-pack adapter in `policy.md`; RADIUS authorization in `radius.md`. Decisions: MDM-01 to MDM-09, with PKI-02, BE-02, BE-03, BE-04, PF-03 and SH-01, in `../decisions.md`; this file resolves C1 and C2 of `../analysis.md`. It settles the MDM items of `../analysis.md` section 3: installer hosting and package formats (section 4), security baseline authoring and the definition of "compliant" with its consumers (section 7 and `../specs/baseline.md`), and the push-certificate signing service with its renewal flow (section 9.4). Claims marked "verify" rest on vendor, community or unconfirmed sources.

Abbreviations: DDM (Declarative Device Management), ADE (Automated Device Enrollment), CSP (configuration service provider), MS-MDE2 (Mobile Device Enrollment Protocol version 2), APNs (Apple Push Notification service), WNS (Windows Push Notification Services), PRK (FileVault personal recovery key), LUKS (Linux Unified Key Setup), CMS (Cryptographic Message Syntax), HPKE (Hybrid Public Key Encryption).

## 1. Device record and lifecycle

### 1.1 `devices` and `mdm` modules

The `devices` module owns what identifies and groups a device: the record (uid, organization, name, OS, hardware serial as an attribute and never as an identity, ownership, labels, lifecycle state with its history), device groups, and device-to-user links. The `mdm` module owns how a device is managed: management channels (Apple MDM enrollment, OMA-DM enrollment), the inventory snapshot, software packages and deployments, desired state for native channels, item results and compliance, the Apple and Windows command queues, escrowed recovery secrets by reference into the secret store (`backend.md` section 6) and the push certificate. `mdm` reads device records through the `devices` Go interface and publishes inventory and compliance facts on the bus; neither reads the other's tables (BE-04).

- Ownership: `corporate` (default), `personal` or `shared`. On `personal` devices wipe and lock are unavailable unless an access policy enables them, and inventory omits per-user application lists.
- Labels: key-value pairs on the record, set by administrators, the API, GitOps or rules over inventory facts (`os.version`, `hardware.model`), which selectors in policy scopes match (`../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 2.1).
- Groups: the `DeviceGroup` resource (`apiVersion: ricevanta.io/v1alpha1`) with `spec.members` (device names) and `spec.selector` (`matchLabels`, `matchExpressions`, `os`). Membership is resolved at bundle compilation (`../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 2.1). Groups also scope RBAC (BE-02) and staged rollouts (AG-07).
- Device-to-user links (BE-01): `enrollment` (the user the enrollment token or MDM enrollment named), `session` (observed interactive logins from the agent's `Session` trait) and `assigned` (an administrator's choice). The primary user is the `assigned` link, otherwise the user with the most interactive time over 14 days. MDM uses the links for user-scoped Apple declarations, notifications and the console. RADIUS takes the user of a device certificate from the `assigned` link, otherwise the `enrollment` link, and never from a `session` link or the derived primary user, since the agent reports sessions and agent state must not become authority for network access (`radius.md` section 2). Shared-device binding belongs to the identity design (`../analysis.md` section 3).

### 1.2 Lifecycle

| State | Entered by | Policy and commands | Certificates (`pki.md`) | RADIUS | Apple MDM and OMA-DM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pending` | Enrollment awaiting approval (PKI-02) | None | None issued | Reject | Not enrolled |
| `active` | Approval or a token without approval | Normal | Issued and renewed | Authorized by identity and compliance | Enrolled, normal |
| `suspended` | Administrator, API or GitOps | Installed bundle stays in force (AG-05); no new assignment; only lock, wipe, message and diagnostics commands dispatch; resume and retire are the permitted transitions | Network-access certificates placed on hold (CRL reason `certificateHold`); identity certificate stays valid so lock and wipe can reach the agent | Reject | Commands limited to lock, erase, restart and queries; no new declarations |
| `retired` | Administrator through BE-02 approval (MDM-02) | Uninstall command with its token (`agent.md` section 9); no bundle; only the removal commands dispatch | Network-access certificates revoked (`cessationOfOperation`) at the transition; each channel credential revoked after that channel completes removal, or its 24-hour timeout (order below) | Reject | Apple `RemoveProfile` for the enrollment profile, OMA-DM `DMClient/Provider/<id>/Unenroll`; each enrollment row closed after that native channel completes removal; timeout remains incomplete |

Suspension is reversible and keeps every enforcement the device already has, because a suspended device is often lost or under investigation; resuming releases the hold and issues a fresh assignment. Retirement is terminal: the uid is never reused, records and events stay for their retention period, and a returning device enrolls as a new record or takes over the old one only under PKI-02 recovery. Retirement first freezes the enrolled agent, Apple and OMA-DM channels and their required cleanup. The server queues each channel's removal and revokes network-access and `user_device` certificates at once. It then tracks `pending`, `completed` or `timed_out` separately for every frozen channel. Agent completion requires the verified signed uninstall result after local management and browser-policy cleanup (`extensions.md` section 7.2). Native completion requires its confirmed removal result or unenrollment check-in bound to that enrollment and retirement transaction; stale check-ins cannot close cleanup. One channel's completion never proves another channel's removal.

The server revokes the agent identity credential only after agent completion and each management credential only after its native channel completes. All unfinished channels retain only removal access until 24 hours after retirement, when their credentials are revoked and their state becomes `timed_out`. The device remains "retired, removal pending" while any required cleanup is incomplete; timeout is never reported as completed retirement. The console names each incomplete channel and offers the signed offline uninstall object for local agent cleanup (`agent.md` section 9), plus the documented local native-unenrollment path. A later audited confirmation can close the remaining cleanup record without restoring its revoked credential. A wipe does not retire the record; the console offers retirement once the wipe result arrives.

## 2. Management channels

| Channel | macOS | Windows | Linux |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent (`/agent/v1`) | Inventory, software, agent-kind items, compliance, scripts, diagnostics, notifications | Primary: inventory, software, agent-kind items, BitLocker escrow, lock, compliance, scripts, diagnostics, update orchestration | The whole MDM |
| Native server | Apple MDM and DDM: profiles, declarations, OS updates, lock, erase, FileVault, activation lock, Recovery Lock, the agent's own package | OMA-DM: settings exposed by a CSP, Windows Update policies, RemoteWipe, Reboot | None |

Windows browser-adapter policy values use the registered vendor channel through the agent (`extensions.md` section 7.2, MDM-03). Every other setting exposed by a CSP documented for Windows 11 Pro, Enterprise or Education goes over OMA-DM; other settings go through the agent with a documented Win32 API; the agent writes no `SOFTWARE\Policies` values except the browser-adapter values in `extensions.md` section 7.2. Those values use the registered browser's vendor-documented registry channel, with exclusive ownership and agent removal; OMA-DM unenrollment alone does not remove them. Windows applies its own MDM and Group Policy precedence to CSP settings, and native unenrollment removes those CSP policies. The agent verifies the effective state of every OMA-DM item for compliance. Apple settings follow a second rule: a DDM declaration where one exists for the setting on every eligible macOS release, otherwise a profile carried by `com.apple.configuration.legacy`; Apple MDM commands only for actions and queries.

## 3. Inventory

The `schemas/policy/osquery-tables.yaml` table map is absent. Pin the osquery release, enumerate supported columns and OS coverage, and add query fixtures before implementing inventory query validation. The schema-contract blockers are listed in `../analysis.md` section 3.

The agent's `Inventory` trait (`agent.md` section 2) collects into tables in `state.db` whose names and columns follow osquery's table schemas for the tables Ricevanta collects, so the osquery-pack adapter (`policy.md` section 5.1, with its table map in `schemas/policy/osquery-tables.yaml`) and `check.query` items (`../specs/baseline.md`) run read-only SQL against them. Queries use the isolated on-demand process and hard bounds of `../specs/rule-adapters.md` section 8.1 for osquery packs, discovery and baseline `check.query`: an inventory-only snapshot and authorizer, 64 KiB values and allocations, a 32 MiB SQLite hard heap limit, a 64 MiB total process cap, bounded function arguments and outputs, at most 1,000 result rows and 1 MiB of results, and a supervised 100 ms end-to-end deadline. A progress handler aborts after 10^6 virtual-machine steps but cannot bound time inside one function. Failed or truncated execution is an error, never an empty passing compliance result. The native launcher, aggregate budget, cancellation and function catalogue remain implementation blockers in `../analysis.md` section 3.

|---|---|---|---|
| Hardware (`system_info`, `cpu_info`, `memory_info`, `disk_info`) | `sysctl`, IOKit registry | WMI `Win32_ComputerSystem`, `Win32_BIOS`, `Win32_Processor`, `Win32_DiskDrive` | `/sys/class/dmi/id`, `/proc/cpuinfo`, `/proc/meminfo`, `/sys/block` |
| OS (`os_version`, `kernel_info`, `uptime`) | `sw_vers` data from `SystemVersion.plist`, `uname` | `RtlGetVersion`, registry `CurrentVersion` | `/etc/os-release`, `uname` |
| Software (`apps`, `programs`, `deb_packages`, `rpm_packages`, `homebrew_packages`, `package_receipts`) | `Info.plist` of bundles under `/Applications` and `/System/Applications`, `pkgutil` receipts, Homebrew Cellar | Uninstall registry keys (both views and loaded user hives), MSIX packages through `PackageManager` | `/var/lib/dpkg/status`, `rpm -qa` with a query format, `flatpak list` |
| Security (`disk_encryption`, `tpm_info`, `secureboot`, `firewall`) | Apple MDM `SecurityInfo`, `fdesetup status` | `Win32_EncryptableVolume`, `Win32_Tpm`, Secure Boot state | `lsblk` and `cryptsetup luksDump` metadata, `/sys/class/tpm`, `mokutil --sb-state` |
| Users and sessions (`users`, `logged_in_users`) | Directory Services, `utmpx` | `NetUserEnum`, WTS sessions | `/etc/passwd`, logind sessions |

Apple MDM queries (`DeviceInformation`, `SecurityInfo`, `InstalledApplicationList`, `ProfileList`, `CertificateList`) fill the same tables for macOS fields the agent cannot read, and Windows `DeviceStatus` and `DevDetail` CSP reads cross-check the agent's values. Extension `collector` modules (EXT-03) add tables declared by their row schemas through the broker in `../specs/extension-agent-runtime.md` section 4; their rows obey the same bounds.

- Schedule: hardware at boot and every 24 hours; software and security tables on change, debounced by 60 seconds, from file events on package databases, `/Applications` and receipt directories, and registry change notifications on uninstall keys, with a full rescan every 24 hours; users on session events; updates every 6 hours and after an update action. Intervals are policy values.
- Delta upload: each row has a hash of its canonical form; the check-in carries added, changed and removed rows since the last acknowledged snapshot sequence, at most 1 MiB per check-in with a continuation flag, and the hash of the full snapshot. A snapshot hash the server cannot reproduce makes it request a full upload.
- Bounds: 20,000 rows per software table, 2,000 per other table, 1 KiB per field (longer values are cut with a truncation marker), 16 MiB per device snapshot. A table over its cap reports `truncated` with the full count, which the console shows. Rows become `inventory_info` and `software_info` events and the `mdm` module's current-state tables.

## 4. Software installation, update and removal

### 4.1 Packages and hosting

A `SoftwarePackage` resource declares one installable artifact: `os`, `format`, `name`, `version`, `sha256`, `size`, the blob store key, `signature` (the expected signer per format below, or `none`, which needs an explicit field and approval), a detection rule (bundle identifier and version, MSI product code, MSIX package family name, uninstall registry entry, package name and version), install and uninstall arguments, `allowedGroups` (the device groups its approval covers, section 4.2), `reboot` (`none`, `required`, `exit-code` mapping) and `interaction` (`silent`, `defer-while-running` with the process list). Deployment is a `Baseline` item of kind `software` with `ensure: present | absent | latest` (MDM-04).

Installers live in the blob store (BE-06), uploaded through the API or console, which computes SHA-256 and size; the bundle carries the hash. Agents download from `/agent/v1/blobs/<sha256>` with HTTP range requests so an interrupted download resumes; Apple MDM manifests point at a `device` role URL with a single-use token. Packages from OS repositories (apt, dnf, zypper, flatpak remotes, Homebrew, winget) come from those repositories or the operator's mirror and are named by repository, package name and version instead of a blob.

| OS | Formats | Integrity check before install | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| macOS | `pkg` (flat package), Homebrew formula and cask (optional source) | SHA-256; package signature through the Security framework (`pkgutil --check-signature` equivalent) with the declared Developer ID Installer team identifier; Gatekeeper assessment for install (`spctl --assess --type install`, verify notarization coverage) | `installer -pkg <file> -target /`; Homebrew as the user who owns the Homebrew prefix, since Homebrew refuses to run as root (verify) |
| Windows | `msi`, `msix`, `exe`; winget (optional source) | SHA-256; Authenticode through `WinVerifyTrust` with the declared subject for `msi` and `exe`; `msix` signature enforced by Windows at deployment plus the declared publisher | `msiexec /i` with `/qn` and a log; `PackageManager.AddPackageAsync` and provisioning for all users (verify the provisioning API); `exe` with declared silent arguments and exit codes; winget in machine scope (verify system-context support) |
| Linux | `deb` through apt, `rpm` through dnf or zypper, flatpak | SHA-256 for local files; `rpmkeys --checksig` against the keys the package declares, since dnf skips local-package signatures by default (`localpkg_gpgcheck`); `deb` files carry no signature by default, so a local `deb` is checked by hash and repository installs by apt's signed `InRelease`; flatpak remotes with GPG verification | `apt-get install -y ./file.deb` with `DEBIAN_FRONTEND=noninteractive`; `dnf install -y`; `zypper --non-interactive install`; `flatpak install --system --noninteractive` |

### 4.2 The software action

Agent software actions are signed commands (`software.install`, `software.update`, `software.remove`) or bundle-driven convergence of `software` items; both use the journal of `../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 7 keyed by command uid or, for convergence, by item and package hash. Idempotency is the detection rule: before acting the agent evaluates it and records `succeeded` without acting when the device already matches. Reconciliation of a `running` row after a crash or reboot: wait while the OS installer lock is held (dpkg lock, rpm transaction lock, the MSI mutex, the macOS installer process), then evaluate the detection rule; a match is `succeeded`; a mismatch retries once only for transactional formats (`msi`, `msix`, `deb`, `rpm`, flatpak, `pkg`) and otherwise becomes `failed` with reason `unknown_state` for an administrator, so an `exe` installer is never blindly rerun unless its package declares `retrySafe: true`. Publishing a `SoftwarePackage` is a protected action (BE-02) whose approval binds the package to its `allowedGroups`, and a signed software command is a protected action. Deployment happens through a `software` item of a `Baseline` and an `mdm` policy, so the compiler refuses a `software` item whose package has `allowedGroups` when the applying policy's scope reaches a device outside them, and it re-checks at every bundle compilation, so a membership change cannot deliver a package outside its approval (`../specs/baseline.md` section 5).

### 4.3 Reboot and user session

A package or update that needs a restart sets the device's `reboot_pending` with its source. Reboots follow the maintenance window and reboot policy of section 6. Before an `interaction: defer-while-running` install, the core asks `ricevanta-session` in each session to show a notice naming the application; the user may defer up to the policy's count and deadline, after which the install proceeds and the notice says so. Installs run as root or LocalSystem in machine scope; a missing session helper never blocks a silent install.

### 4.4 The agent's own package

The agent is installed and updated by `ricevanta-updater` (AG-07), not by section 4.2. On macOS the Apple MDM server also delivers the signed agent package through a `com.apple.configuration.package` declaration with `InstallBehavior` `Required` (macOS 26 and later, supervised) so an MDM-first enrollment receives the agent and a removed agent is reinstalled (verify the reinstall behavior).

## 5. Configuration and management profiles

Desired state is a `Baseline` applied by an `mdm` policy with the `apply` or `report` action (`../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 4); item kinds, channels, drift detection, removal and conflicts are in `../specs/baseline.md` sections 2 and 3.

- macOS: declarations in the device's DDM declaration set and profiles through `com.apple.configuration.legacy`. The enrollment profile and the payloads that require user-approved MDM, among them `com.apple.system-extension-policy`, `com.apple.TCC.configuration-profile-policy` and `com.apple.MCX.FileVault2`, travel only through the Apple channel. Managed Safari extension settings use the `com.apple.configuration.safari.extensions.settings` declaration (supervised; macOS 15 and later) generated from the browser registration (`extensions.md` section 7.2).
- Windows: `windows.csp` items over OMA-DM, among them the Policy CSP areas (`DeviceLock`, `LocalPoliciesSecurityOptions`, `Defender`, `Update`), the BitLocker and Firewall CSPs; `windows.registry` and `windows.service` items through the agent for settings no CSP exposes.
- Linux: the agent kinds `linux.dconf`, `linux.polkit`, `linux.sysctl`, `linux.systemd`, `linux.file`, `linux.repository`, and `linux.pam` for password quality and lockout only, through the distribution's PAM tool, because hand-edited PAM stacks break on distribution updates and can lock every user out.

The legacy Apple update commands (`ScheduleOSUpdate`, `ScheduleOSUpdateScan`, `AvailableOSUpdates`, `OSUpdateStatus`) and the `com.apple.SoftwareUpdate` payload are deprecated in macOS 26 and removed in macOS 27, so Ricevanta uses only the declarations above on every eligible release (MDM-05). Apple's enforcement installs regardless of configured deferrals and retries at the next power-on with network. Profile-based Device Enrollment on macOS 11 and later supervises the Mac, so these supervised-only declarations need no ADE. The target version is the operator's choice or "latest", which the server resolves from the versions eligible devices report as pending; the server never downgrades.

The `os_update` item declares the maintenance window as local-time weekly slots (`days`, `start`, `duration`, `timezone: device | <IANA name>`), evaluated by the agent with the OS time-zone database, since CEL time accessors are UTC-only (`../specs/cel-profile.md` section 1); and a reboot policy (`notify` lead time, `maxDeferrals`, `deadline`). The agent shows the reboot notice through `ricevanta-session`, honors deferrals until the deadline and reboots inside the window. Windows deadlines map to `ConfigureDeadline*` days; macOS `TargetLocalDateTime` is set to the first window slot after the deferral.

## 8. Remote commands and actions

Agent actions are signed commands with the dispatch grant and journal of `../specs/policy-envelope.md` section 7. Apple MDM and OMA-DM commands rest on the `device` role's TLS key and use their protocol's command uid as the idempotency key; protected ones are created only after BE-02 approval with frozen targets.

| Action | macOS | Windows | Linux | BE-02 | Reconciliation of a `running` row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lock | `DeviceLock` with a server-generated six-digit PIN and message; Apple silicon uses the Find My lock and needs the recovery partition | Agent: keep one fresh escrow-acknowledged numerical password protector, enumerate every OS-volume protector with `GetKeyProtectors(0)` and delete every other protector by exact ID, including alternate recovery passwords and external keys; force recovery and restart only after verification (below) | Agent: keep one fresh escrow-acknowledged recovery slot on the managed LUKS2 root and wipe every other exact slot, including TPM2, password, FIDO2, PKCS#11 and additional recovery slots; lock sessions and restart only after verification (below) | Protected above 10 devices | Reconcile the persisted volume, retained route and complete inventory; never delete the retained route or claim success with another route present |
| Unlock | PIN shown to an authorized administrator | Authorized recovery-password read releases the lock generation; after recovery boot, re-enroll the TPM protector and rotate through section 12 | Authorized recovery-key read releases the lock generation; after recovery boot, re-enroll the TPM2 slot and rotate through section 12 | Audited secret read | Resume the persisted release and key-generation transaction; no automatic route recreation while lock remains active |
| Wipe | `EraseDevice` with `ObliterationBehavior` `Default`: Erase All Content and Settings on Apple silicon (macOS 12 and later) | OMA-DM `RemoteWipe/doWipeProtected`, which retries until done and cleans the drive; agent fallback through the `MDM_RemoteWipe` class of the WMI bridge provider (verify) when OMA-DM is down | Agent: `cryptsetup luksErase` on every managed LUKS volume, then power off; the active mapping keeps running until power-off (verify) | Always protected | Repeat is safe and intended; the server waits for the result or the device's silence |
| Restart, shut down | `RestartDevice` with `NotifyUser`, `ShutDownDevice`; or the agent | Agent `InitiateShutdownW` with a reason and notice; `Reboot` CSP when the agent is down | Agent through logind (`systemctl reboot` or `poweroff`), with `shutdown` scheduling for a notice | Protected above 10 devices | Row `running` and boot time after the row: `succeeded`; otherwise `failed` |
| Message | `ricevanta-session` notification; `DeviceLock` message only with a lock | `ricevanta-session` notification | `ricevanta-session` through `org.freedesktop.Notifications` on the user bus | No | At most once: `succeeded` if the helper acknowledged display, else `failed`, never repeated |
| Collect diagnostics | Agent bundle: agent logs, `log show` for Ricevanta subsystems, MDM status | Agent bundle: agent logs, selected event channels, `MdmDiagnosticsTool` output (verify) | Agent bundle: agent logs, `journalctl` for Ricevanta units | No | Regenerate from scratch |
| Run script | Agent: signed script by hash, interpreter, timeout, output cap 64 KiB after secret redaction | Same (PowerShell) | Same (shell) | Always protected | Never rerun unless the script declares `idempotent: true`; otherwise `failed` with reason `interrupted` |

Disk lock is an exact-inventory transaction. Before deleting any route, the agent persists the command uid, recovery epoch, stable volume identity, complete protector or key-slot/token inventory, retained route id, escrow generation and acknowledgement, and deletion progress in the action journal. The retained recovery secret is newly generated for that lock, never released to a user, checked against its installed route and durably acknowledged by `jobs` under section 12. A previously viewed or merely uploaded secret is insufficient. The agent verifies active encryption and refuses suspended BitLocker protection, unrecognized routes, missing escrow, ambiguous volume identity or an unsafe inventory transition before deleting routes.

Windows uses the documented protector inventory and `DeleteKeyProtector` for every non-retained ID; `manage-bde -forcerecovery` alone removes only TPM-related protectors. Linux enumerates all LUKS2 key slots and token associations and uses exact numeric slot removal, not broad type deletion that could erase the retained recovery slot. Reconciliation re-reads the whole inventory before and after each destructive step, stops on an unexpected change and reports `failed` or `unknown_state` with remaining routes. It never disables encryption or erases the last retained route to finish a lock. Before restarting, the agent sends a signed `running` result with reason `recovery_armed` only after the post-removal inventory contains exactly the retained escrowed route with active protection and durable progress. Silence is not a completed lock. A verified post-restart result bound to this lock generation closes the command; qualification must separately prove that boot actually requires the retained recovery secret. Hold encryption convergence and route recreation while the lock generation remains active; only its authorized release permits re-enrollment.

These operations remove current unlock routes. They cannot retract a volume key or LUKS header backup that a hostile administrator copied before the lock, or prevent root or an administrator from defeating management while the volume remains open (`agent.md` section 9). Qualification tests alternate routes, interrupted deletion and concurrent management changes; an unproved transition is a release blocker, not successful lock.

Diagnostics bundles exclude user files, are capped at 100 MiB, pass the secret and identifier redaction of `../specs/ocsf-profile.md` section 3 and upload through `PUT /agent/v1/artifacts/<command_uid>/<part>` as hash-verified zstd parts with a device-signed manifest that `jobs` verifies before the bundle joins the device record (`../architecture.md` section 3.4). Lock on Windows does not work on tablets, where Microsoft documents that `manage-bde -forcerecovery` is unsupported, and Linux lock and wipe need a LUKS2 root the agent manages; both are listed per device as unavailable with the reason (MDM-07).

## 9. Apple MDM server

### 9.1 NanoMDM and the DDM engine

The `device` role serves `/mdm/apple/checkin` and `/mdm/apple/server` with NanoMDM's `http` handlers and `service` layer: the `nanomdm` core service, `certauth` to bind each device identity certificate to its enrollment, and `multi` to fan check-in events out to Ricevanta's own handlers. Storage is NanoMDM's `pgsql` backend in the `mdm` schema; the `device` role's database credentials read queued commands and write check-ins and results only (BE-03). The `api` role creates commands; `jobs` sends pushes. The `device` role's own TLS termination verifies client certificates, and the enrollment profile sets `SignMessage` true, so every check-in carries an `Mdm-Signature` header (verify that the CMS signature is made with the enrollment's identity key). The `device` role stores the exact raw body and `Mdm-Signature` beside the parsed result. `jobs` verifies CMS against the identity certificate bound to the enrollment and derives the parsed fields itself. CMS proves origin, not replay resistance or freshness. Query acceptance additionally binds and consumes the current `CommandUUID`, enrollment, query generation, requested fields and approved required-set revision; repeated bytes cannot refresh evidence (`../specs/baseline.md` section 4.3). `../specs/mdm-evidence-recovery.md` defines the candidate challenge, supersession and consumption records and their qualification boundary.

DDM declaration evidence must match its expected identifier and server token. The native `StatusReport` schema supplies no monotonic report counter or server challenge; its `FullReport` flag describes completeness, not freshness. A raw-body digest prevents exact duplicate consumption but cannot establish the order of a previously unseen withheld pass. The candidate in `../specs/mdm-evidence-recovery.md` binds a signed full report to a no-op status-subscription declaration revision and server token. Apple does not document an on-demand full report or an atomic snapshot across status items. Until every eligible macOS release proves both properties against replay, withholding and reordering, unsolicited native status cannot raise compliance or renew freshness. Unproved required results stay `unknown`; Apple native management remains required for v1.0.0. A fresh signed query can supply evidence only for facts its documented response actually covers, and agent verification does not silently replace required native authority.

The DDM engine is in-project (`backend.md` section 2): it answers the `DeclarativeManagement` check-in endpoints (`tokens`, `declaration-items`, `declaration/<type>/<identifier>`, `status`) for each enrollment, computes the declaration set from the device's resolved baselines plus management declarations (organization information, server capabilities, status subscriptions), stores declarations with server tokens, sends the `DeclarativeManagement` command when a set changes, and turns status reports into item results, inventory rows and events. Whether NanoMDM's `service/dmhook` routes these check-ins is to be confirmed in the first build (verify); otherwise the `multi` service carries them. KMFDDM is an experimental R&D project (MIT) and is not used.

### 9.2 APNs

`jobs` holds the push certificate key and sends `{"mdm":"<PushMagic>"}` with the topic of the push certificate to APNs over HTTP/2 on TCP 443 or 2197, through NanoMDM's `push/nanopush` client; devices reach APNs on TCP 5223. APNs coalesces pushes, so `jobs` sends at most one per device per command batch.

### 9.3 Enrollment profile delivery

Two flows, both ending with one device record and never matching on the serial number (PKI-02):

1. Agent first: the enrolled agent fetches a per-device enrollment profile over `/agent/v1` and opens it for the user, who approves it in System Settings (verify the approval step on each eligible release).
2. Profile first: the user signs in to the enrollment page with an enrollment token and downloads the profile; the MDM server then delivers the agent package (section 4.4) and its enrollment configuration as a managed profile (`pki.md` section 1), bound to the same record by a single-use binding token.

The profile carries the MDM payload (`Topic` equal to the push certificate's topic, which starts with `com.apple.mgmt.`; `ServerURL`; `CheckInURL`; `AccessRights` for every right; `ServerCapabilities` `com.apple.mdm.per-user-connections` and `com.apple.mdm.bootstraptoken`) and an ACME payload for the MDM identity from the device issuing CA with a single-use pre-authorization bound to the record, hardware-bound on Apple silicon (verify minimum release and key types), with a SCEP payload as the fallback (PKI-01). The profile is signed with a server certificate so macOS shows it as verified. Profile-based Device Enrollment on macOS 11 and later supervises the Mac; it remains removable by the user, which `agent.md` section 9 detects. ADE is deferred to v2.0.0 (`../platform-support.md`).

### 9.4 Push certificate signing service (C1)

Each self-hosted server needs its own Apple MDM Push Certificate, requested at identity.apple.com with a request signed by an MDM Vendor CSR Signing Certificate (MDM-08, PF-03).

1. `jobs` creates an RSA 2048 key and certificate signing request (verify the key requirement) and keeps the key under envelope encryption.
2. The operator starts setup in the console, which sends the request, organization name and contact email to the project's signing service at `https://mdm-push.ricevanta.io/v1/requests`. The service verifies the email by a link, signs the request with the project's vendor key and returns the vendor-signed request (a base64 plist carrying the request, its signature and the vendor chain; verify the key names) encrypted to a one-time key the server sent with the request. The service never sees the push private key, keeps the request hash, organization, email and time for its own audit, and rate-limits per email and domain.
3. The operator uploads the signed request at identity.apple.com with a Managed Apple Account or Apple Account, downloads the certificate and uploads it to the console. The server checks that it matches the stored key, records its topic and expiry, and starts pushing.
4. Renewal is yearly: the console warns 60, 30, 14 and 7 days ahead and runs steps 1 to 3 with a new key; the operator chooses "Renew" in the portal with the same Apple Account so the topic stays. The server activates a renewed certificate only when its topic equals the stored one; a different topic needs an explicit re-enrollment choice, since enrolled devices listen on the old topic.

Unreachable service: the current certificate keeps working until expiry, and renewal starts 60 days early to absorb outages. Fallbacks that remove the project dependency: `ricevanta mdm sign-push-request` signs with an operator's own MDM Vendor CSR Signing Certificate, which Apple grants to Developer Program and Enterprise Program account holders on request (eligibility of individual accounts, verify); and any other vendor's signed request is accepted, since the portal checks the vendor signature, not the MDM software. When the certificate expires, pushes fail: devices receive no new MDM commands or declarations until a renewal with the same topic, while the agent channel keeps working, and the console marks Apple-channel features degraded. Qualification proves continuity after renewal (`../specs/platform-qualification.md` section 3, gate 6).

### 9.5 FileVault, activation lock, Recovery Lock

- FileVault: the `encryption` item installs `com.apple.MCX.FileVault2` (with `Defer` and a bypass-attempt limit, since enabling needs the user at login) and `com.apple.security.FDERecoveryKeyEscrow` naming a Ricevanta escrow certificate. The `device` role stores `FDE_PersonalRecoveryKeyCMS` from `SecurityInfo` unopened; `jobs` decrypts it with the escrow certificate key and stores the PRK as a secret (section 12). After any administrator views a PRK, `jobs` sends `RotateFileVaultKey` and stores the new key.
- Activation lock: supervised Macs cannot be activation-locked unless the server sets `ActivationLockAllowedWhileSupervised`, which Ricevanta leaves off by default so a returned device is never stranded. An operator who enables it must have the server fetch `ActivationLockBypassCode`, available only within 15 days after supervision; the server stores it as a secret and reports a device whose code it missed.
- Recovery Lock: `SetRecoveryLock` (Apple silicon, macOS 11.5 and later) sets a per-device password the server generates and escrows, rotated after each view.

## 10. Windows OMA-DM server

- Endpoints on the `device` role: MS-MDE2 discovery (`/mdm/windows/discovery`, and `/EnrollmentServer/Discovery.svc` on `enterpriseenrollment.<domain>` for email-based manual enrollment), MS-XCEP enrollment policy, MS-WSTEP enrollment and renewal, and the SyncML management endpoint, built from Fleet's MIT-licensed code file by file (`backend.md` section 2).
- Enrollment without Entra ID: the agent calls `RegisterDeviceWithManagement` (elevated) with the device's UPN-style identifier, the discovery URL and a single-use security token it fetched over `/agent/v1`; discovery answers `AuthPolicy` `Federated`, and the token is the device security secret in the MS-WSTEP request, so the enrollment binds to the agent's record. Manual "Enroll only in device management" uses the same flow with a token page served as the authentication URL. Imaging uses a provisioning package whose Provisioning CSP `Enrollments` entry carries `OnPremise` with a multi-use imaging token, because one package applied to many imaged devices cannot carry a single-use secret. The token admits OMA-DM enrollment only. Every enrollment made with it stays `pending`: no management certificate is issued and no command dispatches until the record is approved (PKI-02). Binding to the agent's record does not use the serial number: the agent's first check-in presents the OMA-DM enrollment identifier (the `DeviceID` of its MS-MDE2 request, verify) signed by its identity key, and `jobs` merges the pending OMA-DM row into the agent's record. Approval of that record releases the enrollment. Autopilot needs Entra ID and is not used.
- Management certificate: MS-WSTEP certificate requests become `jobs` request rows and are issued by the device issuing CA under a management profile (`pki.md` section 3); the XCEP policy asks for a TPM-backed key through the Microsoft Platform Crypto Provider (verify). Renewal runs through MS-WSTEP before expiry (verify the renewal trigger settings).
- Sessions: the provisioning document sets the `DMClient` poll schedule (default every 8 hours, a policy value). WNS push needs a Microsoft Store app registration, a Microsoft-hosted dependency, so it is not used; when commands are queued, the agent starts an OMA-DM session by running the enrollment's scheduled poll task (verify that the task runs on demand).
- CSPs used: Policy CSP (settings in section 5 and updates in section 6), BitLocker, Firewall, Defender, `RemoteWipe`, `Reboot`, `DeviceStatus`, `DevDetail`, `DMClient`, `CertificateStore`. App installation CSPs are not used, since the agent installs software (section 4).
- BitLocker escrow: Windows backs recovery passwords up only to Entra ID or Active Directory, so the agent reads the numerical password protector through `Win32_EncryptableVolume` (`GetKeyProtectorNumericalPassword`), encrypts it on the device to the escrow certificate's public key (HPKE base mode, RFC 9180, the same code as the RADIUS sealing in `radius.md` section 5; section 12) and sends the ciphertext in its signed check-in; viewing it triggers the acknowledged rotation transaction in section 12.
- Native result authority: OMA-DM has no message signature, so Windows native item results rest on the `device` role, and a compromised `device` replica could write passing `Get` results. The agent verifies the effective state of every OMA-DM item (section 2), and a Windows baseline marks the items the agent can cross-check `authority: agent`, which makes the agent's signed result the authoritative one (`../specs/baseline.md` sections 1.1 and 6).

## 11. Linux

The agent is the whole MDM: inventory (section 3), software and repositories (section 4), configuration through agent kinds (section 5), updates (section 6) and actions (section 8). Native remote wipe is an OS limit (`../platform-support.md`); Ricevanta's wipe is cryptographic erasure of a LUKS2 volume it manages, meaning a LUKS2 root whose key slots include a TPM2 slot and a recovery key the agent enrolled with `systemd-cryptenroll` (systemd 248 and later, which every eligible distribution ships) and escrowed: the agent encrypts the recovery key on the device to the escrow certificate's public key (section 12) and sends the ciphertext in its signed check-in. Enrollment and rotation require the durable acknowledgement in section 12 before an older recovery slot is retired. A root that is not LUKS2 cannot be encrypted in place by Ricevanta, so lock and wipe report unavailable on it and the `encryption` item fails with a reinstall remediation.

## 12. Escrowed secrets

FileVault PRKs, BitLocker recovery passwords, LUKS recovery keys, macOS lock PINs, Recovery Lock passwords, activation lock bypass codes and the push certificate key are secrets under envelope encryption written only by `jobs` (`backend.md` section 6). Reading one needs its own permission, is audited as an `api_activity` event, and triggers rotation where the platform allows it.

The `agent` and `device` roles terminate the signed check-ins and Apple messages, so they never see a recovery secret in plaintext. Each secret that originates on a device is sealed there to the public key of the escrow certificate, which the signed bundle delivers: the agent encrypts the BitLocker recovery password and the LUKS recovery key with HPKE base mode (RFC 9180) and binds the device uid, secret kind and escrow-transaction context below as the HPKE `info`, and macOS wraps the FileVault PRK in CMS to the certificate named in the profile. Only `jobs` holds the escrow certificate's private key (`pki.md` section 3), so only `jobs` opens an escrowed secret, and the other roles store the ciphertext. The escrow certificate carries a P-256 key, which HPKE accepts; if macOS does not accept a P-256 certificate for `com.apple.security.FDERecoveryKeyEscrow`, a second escrow certificate with an RSA key, also held by `jobs`, serves FileVault alone (verify).

BitLocker and LUKS recovery rotation use a durable acknowledgement, not an upload acknowledgement. The agent creates a fresh recovery generation and 256-bit challenge for the authorized command or baseline-convergence transaction, enrolls the new route while keeping the prior usable recovery route, and verifies that the new secret matches that installed route. Its signed escrow submission binds organization, device uid, accepted recovery epoch, transaction uid, command uid or pinned baseline-item binding, stable volume identity, route id, generation, challenge and ciphertext SHA-256. The HPKE `info` binds every preceding identity and transaction field except the ciphertext hash, which is computed after sealing and covered by the device signature. Persist only sealed ciphertext and route metadata locally; never log or store a plaintext recovery key.

Linux numeric slot ids do not provide stable identity across a concurrent header writer. Route retirement remains blocked until Ricevanta excludes every header-writing path across the whole compare-delete interval or qualifies a primitive that atomically compares the expected slot material and header generation while deleting it. Cryptsetup's per-command metadata lock is insufficient.

`jobs` verifies the signed submission, current authorized command or baseline revision, transaction, route binding and report counter, opens the ciphertext, and durably stores the secret with its generation and route metadata. It reads back and opens the committed encrypted record before acknowledging. The authority journal publication barrier (`backend.md` section 6.1) covers the committed generation, transaction and challenge consumption; `jobs` signs only after committed storage passes readback and the journal publication barrier. `jobs` returns a DSSE acknowledgement of type `application/vnd.ricevanta.escrow-ack+json` binding the fields in `../specs/mdm-evidence-recovery.md` plus a per-device `(recovery_epoch, escrow_ack_counter)`. The acknowledgement needs a jobs-only signing key and profile that `api` cannot use. The shared policy and command signing key held by both `api` and `jobs` cannot prove that only the role which opened and verified durable escrow produced it. That jobs-only acknowledgement key and its recovery contract remain unspecified.

Before retiring the prior recovery route or using the new route as the sole disk-lock route, the agent verifies the jobs-only escrow-acknowledgement chain, its accepted epoch, exact transaction, generation, ciphertext hash and fresh stored challenge. It synchronously records the acknowledgement and challenge consumption in the transaction journal before deleting any old route. Retries resume that same transaction and cannot authorize another generation, volume or command. An old epoch, old challenge or unrelated acknowledgement fails; recovery cannot reconstruct authorization from an exposed-role upload or a backup alone.

A lost or delayed acknowledgement leaves the prior recovery route intact and reports rotation pending. A crash after new enrollment, escrow commit, acknowledgement or a journal-proved partial deletion resumes only after a fresh readback-backed retirement authorization. An unjournaled or ambiguous partial deletion stops further mutation and needs a new protected approval bound to the observed partial state. The records and tests in `../specs/mdm-evidence-recovery.md` remain candidates until their schemas, authority barrier, jobs-only signer and fault-injection fixtures are complete. Native FileVault rotation has different command semantics. A compromised TLS-terminating `device` role can acknowledge the HTTP exchange and discard the rotated-key body. Honest crash and retry tests cannot prove safety against that role. FileVault rotation needs an Apple protocol guarantee that prevents key loss or a response-path trust-boundary redesign that the exposed role cannot bypass.

## 14. Agent footprint

The `mdm` module adds no resident process. Idle, it holds row hashes and item state in `state.db`, not in memory, and stays within 2 MB of the core's 44 MB budget (`agent.md` section 6). Collection and item checks are AG-08 work units with CPU budgets (default 5 s per full inventory, 1 s per change-triggered table) and run on change events with debouncing rather than polling; installers run as child processes outside the core's footprint and are metered as processes. Collector modules run in the batch `ricevanta-ext` instance (EXT-03).

## 15. Benefits, trade-offs, dependencies, limits, alternatives

Benefits: the agent gives one management model on three OSes, while the native channels reach what only they can (Apple profiles, DDM updates, FileVault escrow, activation lock and erase; Windows CSP policy and protected wipe); one rule per OS decides each setting's channel; lock on Windows and Linux removes all current alternate unlock routes and retains one fresh acknowledged recovery route; software integrity rests on a signed hash plus the format's own signature; inventory tables in osquery's schema let community packs and `check.query` items reuse existing SQL.

Trade-offs: two native servers add protocol code and the Apple push certificate's yearly renewal; OMA-DM sessions follow the poll schedule or an agent trigger instead of WNS push; disk-encryption lock needs the encryption baseline and a restart; per-item grace and server conditions make compliance richer to explain in the console.

Dependencies: NanoMDM (MIT), Fleet's MS-MDE2 code (MIT, outside `ee/`), the project's MDM Vendor CSR Signing Certificate and signing service (PF-03), APNs, OS package managers, `systemd-cryptenroll` and `cryptsetup`, the Baseline content sources in `../specs/baseline.md` section 5; all with rows in `../licensing.md` or proposed for it.

Limits: Apple unsolicited status freshness and ordering, a jobs-only escrow-acknowledgement signer, an Apple FileVault protocol guarantee or trust-boundary redesign, Linux safe slot retirement, hardware-encrypted BitLocker protector identity, and escrow schemas and crash fixtures remain required mechanism blockers. Disk lock cannot revoke a previously copied volume key or header backup. Apple features need APNs reachability and a current push certificate; profile-based enrollment is user-removable on macOS; activation lock bypass codes exist only within 15 days after supervision; Windows lock needs BitLocker with an escrowed password and is unavailable on tablets; Linux lock and wipe need a LUKS2 root the agent manages; Homebrew and winget are optional sources with weaker integrity than signed installers; a Mac on which the user denies the enrollment profile has no Apple channel and is noncompliant.

Alternatives considered: agent-only management (rejected by MDM-01); KMFDDM as the DDM engine (rejected: experimental, R&D); legacy Apple update commands (rejected: removed in macOS 27); WNS push for OMA-DM (rejected: needs a Microsoft Store app registration, a hosted dependency); the agent writing Windows policy registry keys (rejected: bypasses MDM and Group Policy precedence and leaves settings behind after unenrollment); Windows lock by disabling local accounts (rejected: a local administrator re-enables them and a mistake locks out recovery); serial-number matching between the agent and MDM enrollments (rejected by PKI-02); a server-side wipe that also retires the record (rejected: the wipe result must arrive first).
